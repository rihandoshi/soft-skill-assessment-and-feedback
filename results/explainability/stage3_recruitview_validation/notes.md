## 1. The signals reproduce WavLM, but they mostly rank people, not answers

| | confidence | speaking | overall |
|---|---|---|---|
| WavLM L7 + Ridge (pooled OOF Spearman) | 0.499 | 0.493 | 0.526 |
| Delivery signals, no duration | 0.443 (89%) | 0.451 (91%) | 0.478 (91%) |
| Delivery signals + duration | 0.472 (95%) | 0.481 (97%) | 0.515 (98%) |
| Agreement between independently rated copies of the same recording | 0.55 | 0.34 | 0.57 |

- By the guide's criterion (≥ 80% of WavLM without duration) the categories "capture most of what raters reward".
- **But that is a between-person result.** Within participants, after duration and question type, almost
  nothing survives: only answer length (keep) and speech/articulation rate for confidence (weak, ρ ≈ −0.06).
  The models lean on duration and on voice-identity measures: jitter/shimmer (ICC 0.80–0.85, i.e. mostly
  "who is speaking") are the second-largest contribution after length in Ridge and third (after pace) in EBM.
- WavLM is similar: duration alone explains 28–38% of its out-of-fold prediction; the delivery signals plus
  duration explain 65–66% (cross-validated R²).

## 2. Why the within-participant tests come out empty

The duplicate recordings give a direct estimate of rating noise. The noise variance of a single rating is
about **105% (confidence), 124% (speaking) and 64% (overall)** of the within-participant score variance, with
wide CIs (54 pairs). So most of the difference between one person's answers in RecruitView is rater noise.

Consequences:
- A null within-participant result here does **not** show a delivery behaviour is unimportant; RecruitView
  barely has the power to show it either way.
- What RecruitView *can* show is what raters reward between people, and that is dominated by length and by
  person-level properties (voice, recording setup).
- Per-answer validity of the categories must come from **MIT** (Turker labels such as `SpeakingRate`,
  `Paused`, `EngagingTone`, `Calm`; Stage 7) and the **in-house annotation** (Stage 8). These are now the
  critical stages, not optional extras.

## 3. Optimal bands: not identifiable from RecruitView

Every EBM shape is monotonic over the observed range (slower, steadier, shorter = higher; more pausing =
higher). A monotonic shape has no interior optimum: its "band" is just the end of the data (e.g. the
shortest 5% of answers). Where the shape is noisier (pace, pitch), the band-edge CIs span most of the observed
range. Stage 2 therefore keeps its provisional p25–p75 percentile bands; the shapes are kept as the
*direction* of each signal. The pace band can be revisited on MIT, whose `SpeakingRate` label is two-sided.

## 4. Pausing: our assumption is contradicted between people

More mid-sentence pauses and a higher pause ratio go with **higher** ratings between participants
(EBM increasing; between-person ρ ≈ +0.21 to +0.24), and there is no within-participant relation. This
matches Stage 0. "Hesitation" feedback therefore has no rater support, and the "fewer pauses = better" score
direction is wrong for RecruitView raters. The Stage 2 dashboard score for pausing was built on that wrong
direction, so it is removed.

## 6. What to tell the team

1. The interpretable categories explain ~90% of what the WavLM score model achieves, so the score model is not
   a black box: it largely tracks answer length, pace and person-level voice properties.
2. RecruitView cannot validate per-answer delivery advice: its within-person score differences are mostly rating
   noise. The feedback therefore stays descriptive, and per-answer validation moves to MIT + annotation.
3. Pausing is not a fault for these raters; "speak more slowly" and "be concise" are the only delivery
   directions RecruitView supports, and only weakly within a person.
4. The feedback must never say the score was low *because of* a delivery behaviour (faithfulness R² ≈ 0.65 and
   mostly between-person).
