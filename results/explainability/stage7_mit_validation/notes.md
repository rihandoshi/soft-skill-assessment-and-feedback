# Stage 7 notes: what MIT says about the audio categories

Hand-written interpretation; `report.md` is generated by `validate_categories_mit.py`.

## What was done

- **Label reliability** (`mit_label_reliability.py`): the same 9 Turkers rated all 138 interviews, so ICC(2,k) per label.
- **Answers without diarization** (`mit_segment_answers.py`): MIT audio is one room microphone for both people.
  Verbatim Whisper on each full interview, aligned word-by-word (difflib) to the Turker transcripts, which mark every
  turn Interviewer / Interviewee. Median 99% of ASR words get a speaker; 132 / 138 interviews yield all five answers
  (674 answers; pre and post interviews word the questions differently). No gated pyannote model needed.
- **Unchanged pipeline** (`mit_run_pipeline.py`): Stage 1 / 4 / 5 / 6 / 2 code with RecruitView references, thresholds
  and gate; speaker baseline = the interview's other answers.
- **Pre-registered tests** (`validate_categories_mit.py`): interview level (labels are per interview), 9-rater mean,
  bootstrap CIs over students, partial = controlling mean answer length (not for length measures) and recording
  quality; discriminant matrix; pre / post paired tests.

## Findings

1. **Two labels are unusable.** `SpeakingRate` ICC(2,k) 0.03 and `Paused` 0.26: raters do not agree, so Pace and
   Pausing cannot be validated against their *own* labels. `NotStressed` (0.49) is just below the 0.5 cut-off.
2. **MIT audio is far-field** (speech -40 dBFS, SNR 15 dB, PESQ 1.3): the RecruitView acoustic gate would block 91%
   of answers. Not applied for validation (same room for everyone; quality partialled out in every test). That
   expressiveness validates *on this audio* says the gate is conservative for pitch / energy variability.
3. **Expressiveness: supported, and specific.** Pitch variability vs `EngagingTone` +0.32 / `Excited` +0.29,
   loudness variability +0.46 / +0.42, monotone share -0.32, `monotone` flag -0.16; all CIs exclude 0 after
   length + quality control, and stronger on well-aligned interviews. Pitch variability is strongest on its own
   labels in the discriminant matrix.
4. **Pausing: strongly related to everything (halo).** Mid-sentence pause rate vs `Overall` -0.48, `hesitation` flag
   -0.30, but also -0.55 with `EngagingTone`, -0.59 with `Excited`: fluent speakers are liked across the board.
   Real and large, but not specific to a "pausing" judgement (its own label is unreliable).
5. **Assertiveness: weakly supported, not specific.** Sentence-end pitch rise / `uptalk` flag vs `NotAwkward` -0.22 /
   -0.23 (CIs exclude 0), but similar sizes with `EngagingTone`.
6. **Conciseness: supported.** Longer answers vs `StructuredAnswers` -0.29 (`long_answer` flag -0.30).
7. **Projection (`trails_off`, end-drop): no support.** The flag almost never fires on MIT (1%).
8. **Composure: not supported, and the direction differs by dataset.** Arousal vs `Calm` -0.18 (CI includes 0);
   arousal goes *with* `EngagingTone` / `Excited` (+0.65 / +0.67). RecruitView raters rewarded calm speakers;
   MIT raters reward activated ones. A "calmer than usual" class cannot be phrased as advice either way.
9. **Pace: `too_fast` contradicted.** MIT students are faster than RecruitView (median at RecruitView p62); 22% of
   MIT answers are flagged `too_fast`, and flagged interviews get *higher* `Overall` (+0.18, CI excludes 0). The
   U-shape (distance from the RecruitView median) predicts nothing.
10. **Fillers: Whisper misses ~93% of them.** Turker transcripts: 3.6 fillers per 100 words; Whisper (filler
    prompt): 0.26; per-interview agreement rho 0.16. Transcript filler rate predicts `NoFillers` at -0.61, the ASR
    rate at -0.34. Coaching did not change the actual filler rate (transcripts: 3.7 -> 3.5, p 0.51) although raters
    scored `NoFillers` much higher after coaching (71% improved, p < 0.001): rater expectation / halo.
    `tables/filler_check.csv` is a ready benchmark for the text branch's filler detector.
11. **Pre / post:** after coaching students speak slightly faster (+5 wpm, p 0.016) and more activated (SER arousal,
    p 0.022); `Overall` and `EngagingTone` ratings rise. Pitch / loudness variability and pauses do not change.

## Recommended decisions (not applied; see the summary to the user)

| Category / flag | Now | MIT result | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expressiveness, `monotone` | experimental | supported, specific | promote pitch variability + `monotone` to active (loudness variability stays experimental: quality-sensitive in RecruitView) |
| Pausing, `hesitation` | experimental (withheld on poor audio) | strong but halo; own label unreliable | keep experimental; do not promote on halo alone |
| Assertiveness, `uptalk` | experimental | weak, not specific | keep experimental |
| Projection, `trails_off` | experimental | no support | measurement only |
| Composure | experimental | not supported; direction differs by dataset | measurement only; no composure advice |
| Conciseness, `long_answer` | active | supported | keep active |
| Pace, `too_fast` | active | contradicted between people in MIT | keep but re-examine: population norms do not transfer; phrase relative to the speaker's own pace only |
| Fillers (text branch) | handed over | Whisper misses ~93% | text branch needs a disfluency-aware ASR / detector; validate on `filler_check.csv` |

## Limitations

- Interview-level labels (answers averaged), 138 interviews of 69 students: wide CIs; between-person comparisons.
- Labels from Mechanical Turk viewers watching video: halo is strong (see discriminant matrix).
- Whisper drops many short interviewer interjections, so a few answers may contain a short interviewer prompt.
- 20 interviews align < 80% (transcripts shorter than the recording); the "aligned only" column shows results hold.
