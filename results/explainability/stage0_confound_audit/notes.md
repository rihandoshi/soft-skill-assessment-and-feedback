## 1. Duration → make **Conciseness** a category

| | raw | between participants | within participant |
|---|---|---|---|
| duration vs overall | −0.42 | −0.56 | **−0.12** [−0.18, −0.07] |
| duration vs speaking_skills | −0.38 | −0.50 | **−0.14** [−0.19, −0.10] |

The same person scores lower on their *longer* answers, so length is a behaviour raters penalise, not only a
property of the person. Most of the raw effect is still between people (within/raw ≈ 0.3).

- Conciseness = duration (and word count) relative to the **question-type median**. Behavioural answers are
  naturally longer (median 32 s vs 23–25 s for the other types). The EBM gives behavioural answers a +0.09
  bonus at equal duration, which is the same effect seen from the other side.
- Every other category must still be controlled for duration (percentiles conditioned on duration bucket),
  otherwise they turn into "you talked too long" in disguise.

## 2. Question type affects length, not scores

Mean scores do not differ by question type (Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.73–0.97, also within participants), but
duration does. So question type matters for **norms of length-related signals** (Conciseness, pause counts),
not as a score adjustment. Stage 2 can fall back to duration bucket only for the other signals without losing
much.

## 3. Never absolute advice

| Group | Evidence | Rule for Stage 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Loudness** (`vad_mean_rms`, `rms_energy_*`, `new_*_rms`, dynamic range) | raw −0.38, between −0.52, within −0.10; ICC 0.70. EBM: steep drop up to RMS ≈ 0.035, then flat | Never "speak quieter/louder". Speaker-relative only (e.g. trailing off vs own sentence mean). The small within effect is unexplained. `video_quality` does not explain it (median RMS is the same for Low and High), so test clipping/SNR in **Stage 6** before using it at all |
| **Absolute pitch in Hz** (`f0_hz_*`, `new_f0_*_hz`) | ICC 0.84–0.93: these mostly identify the voice (sex, physiology). Relations are between-person | Only semitones relative to the speaker's median F0 (as already planned in Stage 1) |
| **Raw counts and durations of pauses** (`pause_count`, `*_non_speech_duration`, `pauses_over_*`, max/longest pause) | Raw relations vanish within participants once duration is controlled: they track answer length | Use only as rates or ratios (per minute, per sentence) and percentiles conditioned on duration |
| Voiced percentage, shimmer proxy | Between-person only | Don't use as feedback signals |

## 5. Caveats

- **Within-participant effects are all small (|ρ| ≤ 0.15).** That is expected: within-person variance is
  small and the scores are noisy. But it means per-answer feedback rests on modest effects, and phrasing
  should stay descriptive ("you spoke faster than usual"), not causal ("this lowered your score").
- **A 6-variable EBM (duration, question type, WPM, pause ratio, max pause, RMS) reaches val Spearman 0.49 on
  overall**, about what WavLM L7 + Ridge achieves. Much of that comes from duration and loudness, which are
  largely person/setup effects. WavLM may be exploiting the same confounds. Stage 3's faithfulness check
  (OOF WavLM predictions regressed on duration + RMS) should test this explicitly.
- CIs are not corrected for multiple comparisons (78 features × 3 targets). Trust the patterns that repeat
  across extractors (legacy / `new_` / `vad_` versions of the same quantity), not single borderline cells.
- `video_quality` is constant within each participant (no participant has both), so it can't be separated
  from the person. It is not significantly related to the scores (p = 0.07–0.23).
